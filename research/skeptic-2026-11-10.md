# Skeptic memo, 2026-11-10

## Hypothesis #16

- Why it will fail: A 50/200 cross is a lagging signal. It flips only after most of the +39-47% 3-month rebound has already happened, so entry comes late. Crypto in a 48-52% drawdown regularly produces bear-market rallies that fail, and each failure whipsaws a slow cross (buy high, exit lower). The sample is tiny: only a handful of independent crypto cross events exist, and BTC and ETH are about 0.85 correlated, so it is effectively one bet. The entry stack (above the 200d average, deeply negative 12m return, positive 3m return, low vol) is a post-hoc description of today's state, not a tested condition. The rule has 4-5 conjunctive parameters fitted to one episode. The 'not crowded' claim rests on low volume, which also fits a dead-cat bounce with no real demand. Round-trip fees and slippage on exits at the cross reversal erode a low-frequency edge. The signal is the same trend family as the failed 6-month momentum sleeve and the one-year drift sleeve, so it is likely the same factor with a different lookback.
- Test most likely to break it: Walk-forward backtest on BTC and ETH daily data with the conditions applied exactly as specified (cross flip, 12m return < -30%, 3m return > 0, vol ratio < 1). Charge realistic fees and slippage. Count independent entries; if fewer than about 8-10, or if the deflated Sharpe (with the true trial count from trials.sqlite) is below the gate, kill it. Also compare against plain buy-and-hold of BTC and ETH, and against the failed momentum sleeve: if the return correlation to that sleeve is above 0.7, it adds nothing new.

## Hypothesis #17

- Why it will fail: Breakout-to-new-high on diversified ETFs is a trend-following variant. On liquid index and sector ETFs, 20-day breakouts are heavily arbitraged and the return after a breakout is close to the unconditional drift minus costs. Exiting at the mid-range is a tight stop that sells winners into normal pullbacks. In a rising, moderate-vol market the 20-day high is hit constantly, so the rule trades many times and pays costs and gets whipsawed. The ETFs are highly correlated (QQQ, XLK, SPY), so 'many signals' is really one market bet. The thesis is built on a single month's rally (QQQ +9.5%), which is recency bias. The edge is stated to fail if the market goes sideways, and the entry condition (breakout) is itself most frequent just before range-bound reversals. The rule shares the trend family of the failed trend and momentum sleeves.
- Test most likely to break it: Run a backtest over all us-etf names with the exact rule (20d high entry, mid-range exit, 200d filter, vol filter) with costs of 5-10 bps per side. Measure hit rate, average trade after costs, and turnover. Compare against buy-and-hold of the same basket with equal exposure time (random-entry null with matched holding periods). Kill if the net Sharpe does not beat the null at p<0.05 after the deflated-Sharpe correction, or if the edge disappears in the 2022 and sideways 2015-2016 style regimes.

## Hypothesis #18

- Why it will fail: TLT and IEF are 13-26% below their 1-year highs, which means the bond downtrend is a persistent rate-driven regime, not noise. Buying a 1.5-sigma 10-day fall in a duration trend is catching a falling knife: bond sell-offs are autocorrelated when driven by inflation or term-premium repricing, as in 2022. The claim that 'bonds are mean-reverting more than equities' is asserted, not shown; the 5-day fade of equities and crypto already failed on this market, and this is the same reversal family with a longer window. The z-score exit 'toward zero' is vague and creates open-ended holding periods. The setup requires price within 5% of the 200d average and vol falling, which is a narrow filter that will fire rarely, so there are very few samples. TLT, IEF and LQD are 80-90% correlated, so it is one bet. Carry (yield) in the ETFs can mask negative price drift in the backtest.
- Test most likely to break it: Event study on TLT, IEF and LQD total-return series: condition on 10d return < -1.5 sigma (1-year vol), price within 5% of the 200d average, and 20d vol < 1y vol. Measure forward 5/10/20-day returns against the unconditional mean with a block bootstrap. Split by rate regime (2022 and 2013 taper vs calm periods). Kill if the number of independent events is under about 15, if the conditional forward return is not significantly above unconditional, or if the 2022 subsample is negative.

## Hypothesis #19

- Why it will fail: The thesis contradicts itself. The Why says the trend is intact while the Needs section requires the 3m change to be negative, which is a pullback in a trend. The signal is a 12-1 momentum on just two assets (XLE and DBC), which is a time-series bet on a single sector/commodity factor. A 38.6% 12-month ratio gain is already an extended move, and energy momentum reverses sharply when oil mean-reverts. The note that DBC/GLD 'does not matter' dismisses a check that was apparently inconvenient. XLE and DBC are both oil-heavy, so the diversification that inverse-vol sizing needs is absent, and with two assets the sizing is almost an equal-weight oil bet. Energy is also a fenced area (oil) for live use, so any result could not be deployed. It is the same trailing-drift factor as the failed one-year drift sleeve and the 6-month momentum sleeves. The trade is justified by an inflation-shock story with no inflation data tested.
- Test most likely to break it: Backtest 12-1 time-series momentum on XLE and DBC alone with inverse-vol weights, with costs, over every available year. Check the return correlation with the failed one-year drift sleeve (kill if above 0.7). Check the sign of the 3m<0 and 12m>0 pullback subset separately: if the pullback subset's forward 1-3 month return is not positive in more than 55% of events, or if the Sharpe is driven by one oil-shock episode (2021-2022) so that dropping that year leaves a Sharpe under 0.3, kill it. Also flag the oil fence before any deployment.

## Hypothesis #20

- Why it will fail: This is nearly a duplicate of #18 (TLT and LQD after a sharp fall) with a different filter set, so it counts as a second trial on the same hypothesis and inflates the multiple-testing penalty. The conditions conflict: the 'Why' says TLT/IEF is 13% down over 12 months, yet a >1 sigma 10-20 day decline is required, and a sell-off in long bonds that is not a credit event usually means rising real rates, which persist. The stock-bond correlation band (positive but below 0.3) has no demonstrated link to forward bond returns; it is an arbitrary threshold and adds a parameter for no known mechanism. A 60d correlation is noisy, with a standard error of around 0.13, so the 0.3 cut-off is statistically indistinguishable across many days. The one-sigma threshold is low and will fire very often, so the trade is mostly a general long-duration bet, which loses in a rising-rate regime. The 'LQD/IEF above 200d' filter is almost always true in calm markets, so it filters little.
- Test most likely to break it: Compare it to #18 on the same data: if the entry dates overlap more than 50% or the returns correlate above 0.8, count it as one idea. Run an ablation: drop the correlation filter and the credit filter one at a time. Kill if removing the correlation band does not reduce performance (the filter is noise) or if the conditional 10-20 day forward return of TLT/LQD is not significantly different from unconditional after block bootstrap. Check the 2022 subsample explicitly.

## Hypothesis #21

- Why it will fail: It is the same trend-following family as the failed trend and six-month momentum sleeves, with a different lookback (50/200 cross instead of 6-month return); the claim that it differs is cosmetic. XLK, QQQ and XLF are heavily overlapping (QQQ is about 50% tech), so there is one long-equity bet that is mostly market beta. Long-only equity beta in a rebound will look good in any backtest that spans a recovery, so the result is beta, not skill. The risk-on confirmations (XLK/XLU, LQD/IEF above the 200d average) are all lagging, so by the time all three conditions are true, the rebound is mostly done. Three conjunctive filters and a 'down 16% over 12 months' narrative mean the setup is tuned to today's picture. A 50/200 cross is known to whipsaw in sideways markets, and the equity sample (a few cycles) is too small to separate it from buy-and-hold. The confirmation filters add parameters without evidence they improve outcomes.
- Test most likely to break it: Backtest the cross rule on XLK, QQQ and XLF with and without the confirmation filters, with costs, against buy-and-hold at matched average exposure. Regress the strategy returns on SPY: kill if the alpha t-stat is below 2 or if Sharpe is not above matched-exposure beta. Check the correlation with the failed trend and momentum sleeves (kill if above 0.7). Compute the deflated Sharpe with the full trial count; kill if it is below the gate or if the ablation shows the confirmation filters do not add value.
