# The working stop, the stake ladder, fractional live orders and the unattended switch

Date: 2026-11-14. Status: approved to build by the owner ("Yes, all four, and build the caps design"). Nothing here sends a real order; nothing here is cleared for real money.

## 1. What the owner approved, in his terms

- **His wall, in files only he writes:** the daily loss halt (2%) and the drawdown pause (25%) in `config/risk.json`; the money ceiling, the largest order and a monthly renewal in `config/webull_live.json`; the checklist in `config/go_live.json`. No code writes these files.
- **A working daily stop** the system sets for itself each day: 2.5 times the book's daily swing over the last 25 sessions, never under 1%, never over his daily halt.
- **A ladder** that moves the stake at work under his ceiling, never the ceiling:
  - step 0, 20 sessions: every send needs his ticket approval, as built today;
  - step 1, 40 sessions: unattended at the same stake;
  - after that the stake doubles after every 60 clean sessions, up to his ceiling;
  - a fault halves the stake;
  - a 2% market day only pauses that day;
  - the 25% pause stops everything until he reviews, then resumes one step down.
- **Fractional live orders**, so a small first stake can hold the whole book.
- **The unattended switch**, his own.

Still his, no number approved: the money ceiling, the first stake, orders a day, the largest order, the checklist's figures.

## 2. Decisions made by the lead inside that approval (each is a reading, flagged to the owner)

**D1. One new owner file, `config/ladder.json`.** Read only through `venues.webull.read_owner_file` (same owner, permission, link and folder rules as the other owner files). Exactly these keys: `confirmed_by` (a name), `first_stake` (a finite number above 0), `unattended` (true or false), `fractional` (true or false), `valid_until` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, not past, at most 31 days ahead of the New York date: the same rule as the confirmation). Any other key, a missing key or a wrong type is a refusal in one line. `first_stake` above the confirmation's `max_capital` is a refusal. **With no such file, live mode behaves exactly as it does today** (tickets, `--capital` by hand). No program creates or edits this file. Its content joins the digest a live ticket is bound to, in the same canonical form the other owner files use (a change of meaning invalidates a ticket; a change of spacing alone does not).

**D2. The ladder is computed, never stored.** Its position is a pure function of the journal's entries for the live venue, the exchange calendar and the two owner numbers (`first_stake`, `max_capital`). There is no ladder state file to damage or to edit. The same journal a person can read explains every step.

**D3. What a session is.** A New York date that is an exchange session (XNYS) on which the live venue has at least one `order_step` summary. Each session is exactly one of:
- **fault**: any of its summaries stopped at a stage in `FAULT_STAGES = {"state", "reconcile", "send", "after", "journal", "baseline", "unexpected"}`, or any of its summaries carries a non-empty `faults` list (kinds in D8);
- **clean**: not a fault, and at least one summary has status `done`;
- **skipped**: everything else (only ordinary or data stops: `session`, `ticket`, `lock`, `live`, `clock`, `environment`, `targets`, `follow`, `prices`, `marks`). A skipped session neither counts nor resets the count.

This reads the owner's "a stopped run" as "a run the system's own error or a records mismatch stopped". A run stopped because a price could not be confirmed, or because his own file lapsed, is a skipped day: halving the stake on a data outage would sell half the book for no fault of the book.

**D4. The position.** `(mode, stake, clean, needed)`, starting at `("ticket", first_stake, 0, 20)`.
- a clean session adds 1 to `clean`. When `clean == needed`: in mode `ticket` the mode becomes `unattended` with the same stake and `needed = 40`; in mode `unattended` the stake becomes `min(2 * stake, max_capital)` and `needed = 60`; `clean` restarts at 0. A stake already at the ceiling stays there.
- a fault session: `stake = stake / 2`, `clean = 0`; the mode and `needed` are unchanged. (Literal: no floor. The order ledger's own blocks, not the ladder, are what stops sending after a fault.)
- a drawdown pause followed by the owner's reset (D7): **one step down**, `clean = 0`: mode `unattended` with `stake > first_stake` becomes `stake = max(first_stake, stake / 2)` (`needed` is 60 when the new stake is above `first_stake`, else 40); mode `unattended` with `stake <= first_stake` becomes mode `ticket`, same stake, `needed = 20`; mode `ticket` stays. A step down never goes below the first stake (a stake held at a ceiling of 150 on a first stake of 100 steps down to 100, the step it came from; plain halving would have left 75, and a LOWER ceiling a LARGER stake). Only a fault halves without a floor. Between the pause and the reset no CLEAN session counts; a fault session halves the stake and zeroes the count on any day, paused or not, the pause's own day and the reset's own day included (a day that both faults and trips the pause costs both: the halving, then the step down at the reset). A pause and a reset are events, not sessions: they count on whatever calendar day they carry, a weekend or a holiday included, and take effect before the next session. **Within one day (as built after two blind checks, 2026-11-14):** a clean or skipped session comes before the day's events; a fault takes its place among the day's pauses and resets by the times of its faulty summaries; and where the times do not tell the order of a day's fault, pauses and resets, the ladder carries EVERY position the record allows through all later days and reports the lowest stake, ticket mode where any of them has it and the pause where any of them has it, so that the report is never above any possible real order (more than 720 possible orders on a day or 64 positions is a refusal: a person must look).
- **the ceiling is a fact of each day, not a setting replayed over the past.** Each live summary records `ceiling`, the owner's `max_capital` in force when that run read his file. In the walk the stake is first held to that day's ceiling, and a doubling is `min(2 * stake, that day's ceiling)`; a day with no recorded ceiling can never raise the stake. Today's ceiling is applied once, at the read: `stake = min(walked stake, max_capital)`. So a lowered ceiling lowers the stake at once and can never raise it, a raised ceiling raises nothing by itself, and the mode and the counts never depend on today's ceiling. (Replaying the whole history under today's ceiling, the first design, let a ceiling lowered by one dollar double the stake sooner after a pause: found by the builder's own aimed search.)
- a session whose day stop tripped (D6) is clean or fault by D3 like any other: a market day is no fault.
The function also returns the list of changes, each with its day and its reason in words.

**D5. The working stop.** `working_stop_pct = min(wall, max(1.0, 2.5 * s))` where `wall` is `max_daily_loss_pct` and `s` is the sample standard deviation (n - 1), in percent, of the last 25 **exact** daily returns of the followed strategies taken together in the paper book (the record the decay monitor already reads; a bridged observation is not exact). Fewer than 25 exact returns: `min(wall, 1.0)`. The result is never above the wall and is reported with its basis ("25 returns ending D" or "fewer than 25 returns: the floor").

**D6. A stop that lasts the day.** When the day's loss at any look is at or above the working stop, the run's summary carries `day_stop` (`{"pct": loss, "limit": working stop, "wall": true or false}`) and non-reducing orders are refused for the rest of that New York day, by this run and by any later run that day (read back from the journal). Reducing orders still pass. Nothing carries to the next day. The base of "the day's loss" is unchanged (the last run of an earlier day).

**D7. The 25% pause for the live venue.** Today only the paper trader creates a latch. Live gets its own: `journal/kill_switch-webull-production.json`, created by the order step when the live drawdown reaches `max_drawdown_pct`, journaled as an entry of type `drawdown_pause`. While it exists every live run stops before any order, at the stage `lock` (a stage the ladder skips: a run stopped by the pause is not a fault, or every paused day would halve the stake again); the paper latch keeps its present effect. Only the owner clears it: `reset-kill-switch --venue webull-production --who NAME` removes the file, journals `latch_reset` and restarts the live peak from the next recorded equity. The ladder reads those two entries.

**D8. Fault kinds a run can carry** (`faults` in its summary, written only when not empty; as built and corrected through three blind checks, 2026-11-14): `reconcile` (the run's final reconcile was not clean for a reason other than a position difference in a symbol the run has just traded or that still has an open order, positions that could not be read, or the unread status of an order known to be working: those are named in `reconcile_pending`, and the next run's first reconcile judges again); `attention:<kind>` (a blocking attention item was opened in this run); `cap` (a fill that broke what was checked: one order filled for more units than it asked, or an order's average fill price on the wrong side of its limit, each judged when a run first records the fill, whichever run sent the order; on live runs also a purchase fill above `max_order_value`); `drift` (D10). A book above `max_capital` after a run is NOT a fault: the per-order check already refuses any buy that would cross the ceiling, so a book found above it is a market gain or the owner's own lowered ceiling; it is recorded as `over_ceiling`. The two questions every fault rule must answer together: can a day on which nothing went wrong write this entry, and can a real fault of this kind go unrecorded.

**D9. Unattended sending.** A live run with neither propose nor approve is allowed only when: `config/ladder.json` is valid with `unattended: true`; the position's mode is `unattended`; every lock is met. It is done as a propose and an approve inside one process (the ticket is made, bound to the same digests, and used at once with the fate `used (unattended)`), so the ticket book stays the single record of what was sent and why. Every other check of a live run is unchanged.

**D10. Live against paper ("drift").** Each live summary records `book_return` (the live book's return for the day from the order ledger's fills and marks, fees included; immune to deposits and withdrawals) and `paper_return` (the followed strategies' paper return for the same day). **The drift fault ships switched off** (`DRIFT_ACTS = False`): its limits cannot be honestly set before the practice venue has produced at least 20 sessions of the same two numbers. Until then drift is measured and shown, not acted on. The owner is told that this part of "clean" is not yet live. (Corrected 2026-11-14: a run in the morning cannot know the day's return, so no summary carries these two numbers; they are worked out afterwards from records already kept, the fills, the closes and the paper record, in a ticket of their own, and can be worked out for every practice day back to the first.)

**D11. Capital and fractions under the ladder.** With a valid `config/ladder.json`, the capital of a live run is the ladder's stake; `--capital` must be absent or equal to it. Fractional live units are allowed only when the file says `fractional: true` AND the journal holds at least one fill of a non-whole quantity on the PAPER venue whose batch's summary is clean (done, and carrying no fault: a batch whose records did not match the broker proves nothing), so the broker's handling is proven in its test environment first. The ticket binds the fractional flag as today. Production's own handling of fractions stays unknown until the first approved ticket: a refusal there is an ordinary refused order.

**D12. Not in this build.** A working number of orders a day under his cap; the bots' veto or shrink; both key pairs side by side; any change to the paper trader.

- With it: `order-step --live --check` prints the position (mode, stake, clean sessions of how many, the next change), the working stop and its basis, and whether fractions are allowed and why.
